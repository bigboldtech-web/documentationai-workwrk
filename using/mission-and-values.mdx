WorkwrK treats your **mission** and **values** as a live part of the product, not a page nobody reads. Set them once and they run through the app: a mission greets the team when they open WorkwrK, values fill the loading screens as people work, and recognition is given for living a specific value.

---

## Setting the mission and values

Go to Workspace settings, **Identity & culture**, on the **Culture** tab: the **Mission and vision** card and the **Values and the welcome splash** card. Owners and Admins can edit them.

- **Mission**: one line, the goal everyone follows.
- **Values**: add as many as you like, one at a time. Type a value and press Enter or **Add**, and remove one with the `x` on its chip.

The same profile also grounds AI generation of KRAs and KPIs, so a richer profile makes better suggestions there too.

<Callout kind="info">
  Until a mission is set, the welcome and loader screens simply show the plain WorkwrK dots, so nothing looks broken on a workspace that has not filled this in yet.
</Callout>

---

## The welcome screen

When someone opens WorkwrK, a brief full screen moment shows the company mission (and a value) before the workspace appears. It is short and can be skipped with a click or the Escape key. The workspace loads behind it, so it never holds anyone up.

## Values fill the loading screens

Wherever the app pauses to load, moving between sections, opening a panel, a page fetching its data, the loader shows one of your mission or values instead of a bare spinner. They take turns one by one, so over a week the whole team sees each value repeatedly. During an active session a fresh one appears roughly every ten minutes rather than on every single click, so it reinforces without getting in the way.

---

## Recognising a value (Kudos)

Values are not only shown, they are something people give each other credit for. When you send **Kudos** to a teammate, you can tag it with a company value: the value chips in the Kudos composer are **your** configured values.

The Kudos page then rolls this up:

- A bar of your values with a count of how often each has been recognised, which doubles as a filter.
- A leaderboard of who has received the most recognition, and the values they are most often recognised for.

So the values you set are not posters on a wall. People see them, live them, recognise each other for them, and you can see which ones actually show up in the work.
