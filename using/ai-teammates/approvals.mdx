---
title: Approvals and Don't ask
description: What an AI teammate asks you first, how to approve, change or deny a request, and how to let it act without asking.
---

An AI teammate works as you. So before it does something other people will see, it stops and asks you. Its request waits on an **approval card** until you approve it, change it or deny it.

## What asks first

The **Tools** list, in the new teammate form and on the **Tools and approvals** tab, sorts every tool into four groups. The group sets what happens by default.

| Group | By default | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Look things up** | Never asks. Reading changes nothing. | Find tasks, Find people, Find goals, Read your Inbox, Read your Talk messages |
| **Make and change your own work** | Runs at once. You can choose **Ask me first**. | Create tasks, Change tasks, Comment on tasks, Draft SOPs, Schedule meetings, Create goals, Remember things, Set up routines |
| **Things other people will see** | Asks first. You can choose **Don't ask**. | Create docs, Create forms, Create tables, Move tasks between Lists, Post in Talk, Send kudos, Create KRAs, Create KPIs |
| **Always asks first** | Always asks. **Don't ask** is never offered. | Invite people |

A tool in the second group still asks first when one particular use of it reaches other people:

- a task it creates for someone else (it goes on their Personal list),
- a change to a task, or a comment on it, unless the task is on your own Personal list and nobody else is on it,
- text it adds to a doc other people can open (anything but your own notepad),
- a meeting with anyone else invited,
- a goal owned by someone else, or a Department or Company goal.

A new doc, form or table asks first because everyone in the workspace can open and edit it.

## The approval card

A card shows **Waiting for you**, then what the teammate wants to do, in its own words: for example, **Post in #general** or **Create task "Call Acme" for Max Chen**. Under the title you see:

- the exact text that will be posted, commented or added (long text shows **Show all**),
- facts about who it reaches, such as **34 people can read it.** or **The task's assignees and watchers are told.**,
- how to undo it, where that is possible, such as **Earlier versions are kept in the doc's history.**,
- **Waits until** followed by the date it expires.

What runs is exactly what the card shows. The card is built from the request itself, not from the teammate's description of it.

Choose one of:

- **Approve**: it runs now.
- **Edit**: change the one field the card lets you change (the title of a task, meeting or goal, a comment, a Talk message, the text added to a doc, or a kudos message), then choose **Approve with changes**, or **Cancel**. Not every card has **Edit**.
- **Deny**: nothing happens. The chat records **You said no:** followed by the title.
- **Approve and don't ask again**: approves it and stops the teammate asking you for this kind of action again. It is shown only where that choice is allowed. For a post in Talk it names the conversation, for example **Approve and don't ask again in #general**, and covers that one conversation only.

### Several requests at once

When one answer asks for several things, one card lists them all: **3 things are waiting for your approval**. Each has a tick box, all ticked to start. Untick any you want to hold back, or use **Select all**, then choose **Approve 3** or **Deny 3**. Open a title to see its text and facts, and use **Edit** on any one of them.

An invitation never joins the batch. It always has its own card, so you approve it on its own.

### After you decide

The card changes to **Approved**, **Denied**, **Expired**, **Didn't work** or **Cancelled**, with a line saying when, and an **Open** link to what was made or changed.

When you approve in the chat and something ran, the teammate carries on from where it stopped. Carrying on uses one AI question. Denying never does: the teammate hears what you decided the next time you message it.

## Where requests wait

- **In the chat** where the teammate asked.
- **Waiting for you**, the tab on the AI teammates page that lists only teammates with something waiting.
- **The AI sidebar**: the **AI teammates** row counts what waits for you.
- **Your Inbox**, for requests made while you weren't in the chat: a routine's run, an answer in Talk and an automation step each write one row, for example **Chief of Staff is waiting for your approval**. Open the row and the approval card opens right there, so you can approve, change or deny it without leaving the Inbox. **Open chat** takes you to the chat. Deciding from the Inbox does not make the teammate carry on.

A request asked by one teammate on another teammate's behalf waits in the chat of the teammate that will do it. See [Group chats and asking other teammates](/using/ai-teammates/group-chats).

## When a request ends without you

- **Expired**: a request nobody answers expires after 7 days. The card tells you to ask the teammate again if you still want it.
- **Cancelled**: the teammate was removed, can no longer use that tool, or you left the group chat it was asked in.
- **Didn't work**: you approved it but it failed, for example because you no longer have access. The card says why.
- **Couldn't confirm it finished**: rarely, a request that started may not have finished. It never runs again by itself. Check the task, doc or conversation before asking again.

If a teammate is paused, its waiting requests can't be approved until it is turned on again. They still expire after 7 days.

## Choose Don't ask

**Don't ask** lets a teammate do one kind of thing without stopping for you. You can choose it:

- in the new teammate form, beside each tool,
- on the **Tools and approvals** tab of the teammate's settings,
- from an approval card, with **Approve and don't ask again**.

Some limits always apply:

- **Invite people** always asks.
- **Post in Talk** has no tool-wide Don't ask. You can only choose it for one conversation at a time, from the card of a post there.
- A tool-wide **Don't ask** covers only your own work. The uses listed under [What asks first](#what-asks-first) that reach other people keep asking until you choose **Approve and don't ask again** on one of their own cards. The tab then shows **Doesn't ask, even when other people will see it** for that tool.
- When a teammate answers in Talk, runs in an automation, or is asked by another of your teammates, it asks you first for everything other people would see, whatever you chose.
- On a workspace teammate, an Owner or Admin can turn on **Ask everyone first** for a tool. That holds the tool at **Ask me first** for everyone, and the tab says **Whoever manages it set this to ask everyone first.**

Your choices are yours alone. On a workspace teammate, nobody else's **Don't ask** applies to you, and yours applies to nobody else.

### Change your mind

Open the teammate's settings, then **Tools and approvals**. Change any tool back to **Ask me first**. Choices stored from approval cards are listed under the tool, such as **Doesn't ask in #general**, each with **Remove**.

The footer of the tab sums it up: **Reading never asks. Anything other people will see asks first unless you choose Don't ask. Inviting people always asks.**

## Ask AI asks first too

Ask AI uses the same approval card. Before anything other people would see, such as kudos, an invitation, a new doc, form or table, or a task for someone else, it shows a card and waits. Your own work, like a task for yourself, still happens at once. Ask AI never offers **Approve and don't ask again**, and its requests also expire after 7 days.

## Limits

- One answer can ask for up to 50 things.
- Up to 100 requests can wait for one person at once. Past that, the teammate tells you too many things are waiting for your approval, and you need to decide some first.
- One answer can take up to 30 actions. Past that the teammate says **That's the most actions one turn can take. Send another message to carry on.**
