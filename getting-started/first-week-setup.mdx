title: First-week setup
description: The ordered checklist an admin follows to stand WorkwrK up, and what each step unlocks downstream.
---

This is the order to build in. Each step is a prerequisite for the one after it, and doing them out of order produces the two failure modes we keep hitting: people who land in the workspace with no job title, and KRAs that belong to nobody.

You need to be an Owner or Admin, or a Member with a manager tier, to do most of this.

---

## Step 0: know what you are building toward

By the end of the week you should be able to open any person's record and see: their job title, the department it sits in, the result areas that title owns with weights, the gauges under those areas, and their goals. If any of that is blank, an earlier step was skipped.

---

## Step 1: create your departments

**Where:** **Teams**, **Departments**, or Workspace settings, **Structure**, **Departments**.

Choose **New department** and fill in the dialog: a name, and optionally a description, a colour, a department head and a parent department. The page shows a tree with headcount per department, a search box, and a Grid toggle.

Department names are unique per workspace, so a second department with the same name fails to create.

**What this unlocks:** a department is the container you will hang job titles from. Goals can be assigned to a whole department as a contributor group, and that assignment is worked out from today's members every time, so a new hire inherits the department's goals automatically.

**What you can change later:** everything except a merge. Rename, edit the description and colour, assign or change the head, re-parent into a nested tree, and delete. The server refuses to delete a department that still has people in it, so reassign people first. If you end up with two departments that should be one, move the people across by hand and delete the empty one.

---

## Step 2: create your job titles

**Where:** **Teams**, **Job titles**.

Create a job title with its title. Its page opens, which is where the rest is filled in. That is deliberate: it stops half-defined titles from piling up in the list.

On the job title's page, fill in the **Details** card:

- **Description**: one line on what this job title is for and what it must ensure.
- **Department**: pick the department. This is the field that says "Sales" or "Operations".
- **Seniority**: where the title sits, for grouping the list. It does not change what anyone can open.

**What this unlocks:** everything downstream. KRAs attach to job titles, people attach to job titles.

Create a job title for every distinct job you have, including yours. Two people doing genuinely different jobs need two titles even if they report to the same manager.

A job title can be renamed from its Details card at any time, duplicated with **Duplicate as new job title**, and deleted with **Delete job title** once nobody holds it and it defines no KRAs.

---

## Step 3: give each job title its KRAs, with weights totalling 100

**Where:** the job title's page, in the **KRAs & KPIs** card. You can also start from **Teams**, **KRAs & KPIs**, which is a job-title picker that routes you to the same place.

For each result area this job owns:

1. Choose **Add KRA**.
2. Name it as a permanent area of responsibility, not a target. "Vendor quality", not "hit 95% by March".
3. Set the **Weight %**: the share of this job that this area represents.

Aim for the weights to total 100. The card shows the sum and says **Aim for 100%**.

**Honest note:** nothing stops you saving a job title whose weights sum to 80 or 130. Each individual weight is checked and that bound is a hard reject; the total is not. A save that takes the title past 100 goes through with a warning naming the running total, rather than refusing. Check the sum before you move on.

**What this unlocks:**

- **Inheritance.** Every person who already holds this job title inherits the KRA the moment you add it, at the weight you set. It is not only applied to future hires.
- **Per-person weights.** Each holder gets their own copy starting at the title's weight, editable per person afterwards.
- **A place to hang gauges.** KPIs live under KRAs.
- **A place to hang procedures.** SOPs attach to a KRA, and published ones under a title's KRAs are assigned automatically to its holders.

If you already have KRAs floating around that belong to no job title, they show up in a **Needs a job title** section on the KRAs & KPIs page. Attach them; they are never auto-assigned or deleted.

---

## Step 4: add KPIs under each KRA

**Where:** same card, **Add KPI** on each KRA.

For each gauge, set:

- **Name**, and a definition saying exactly what is counted and where the number comes from. Write the definition. It is the difference between a gauge people trust and a gauge people argue about.
- **Direction of good**: higher is better, lower is better, or hold the line.
- **Measurement**: **Quantitative**, a number, or **Qualitative**, a 1 to 5 rating with labelled anchors.
- **Recorded**: daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly or annually. The recorder converts a target set at one cadence into the target for the period being recorded.
- **Healthy line** and **Baseline today**, plus the **Unit**. Leave the healthy line blank if you genuinely do not have one yet; the gauge then reads "no baseline yet" rather than showing a made-up number.
- **Shared gauge**: off means Owned, the job title controls the number and it is graded. On means Shared, the title influences it and it is reviewed rather than graded. Be honest here, because grading someone on a number they cannot move is how the whole system loses credibility.
- **North-star gauge**: optionally flag the gauge that matters most for this KRA. Keep it to one per KRA by convention; nothing enforces it.

**What this unlocks:** KPI recording on every holder's record, the manager review queue, and the ability for a goal's target to be measured by the gauge instead of by hand.

## Step 5: create at least one SOP

**Where:** the **Docs** hub, **SOPs**, then **New SOP**.

The picker offers four kinds, and the choice is made once at creation:

- **Written SOP**: long-form prose in the block editor, with its own description field. Best for policies and runbooks.
- **Step-by-step SOP**: an ordered list of steps with per-step notes and screenshots. Reads like a recipe.
- **Checklist SOP**: discrete steps someone ticks off, and the only kind that runs as a process run with a share link.
- **Click-capture SOP**: click through the task and the recorder extension writes a screenshot and a step for each click.

Once it exists, two things are worth doing straight away: set its **Linked KRA** on the detail page so it flows with the job title, and **Publish** it, because only published SOPs auto-assign to a title's holders.

Assignees close an SOP out with the **Mark as read** button in its header, which acknowledges it and completes their assignment. That acknowledgement is what the SOP read rate on the Alignment board counts.

## Step 6: invite your people

**Where:** **Invite** on the Directory, or Workspace settings, **Members**, **Invite**.

The dialog collects:

- **Email addresses**: a chip input, comma, space or Enter separated. One invitation is sent per email.
- **Access level**: the role and tier they start with. The Owner role is never offered.
- **Department**, **Role** and **Reporting manager**: all optional, and all carried on the invitation so the hire lands placed instead of floating.
- **Personal message**: optional, quoted in the invitation email.

**Set the Role in the dialog if you can.** The role is the definition: its KRAs, KPIs and published SOPs attach automatically when the person joins. If you leave the placement fields blank, step 7 becomes mandatory for that person.

Every invitation takes a seat and must go to one of your allowed email domains. See [People and invites](/admin/people-and-invites).

## Step 7: check each person is placed into their job title, department and reporting line

**Where:** **Teams**, **Directory**, open the person, then **Edit details**. Owners, Admins and the People team can change placement; everyone else sees the record without the edit control.

Set, or confirm:

- **Department**
- **Job title**
- **Reports to** (their manager)
- **Office**

**What this unlocks, and why it is the most important step in the week:**

- **Setting the job title seeds their alignment.** The moment you save a new job title on a person, their KRAs are seeded from that title's templates at the title's weights, along with the published SOPs sitting under those KRAs. Re-saving the same title does nothing harmful.
- **Setting Reports to builds the org chart and the manager's view.** Until a person has a manager, no manager can see their alignment data, their goals will not appear in a manager's list, and they will not show in the org chart under anyone.
- **Setting the department makes department-level goal assignment work** for that person.

A person with a job title but no manager is visible only to the People team, Directors and above, and Admins. A person with a manager but no job title has no KRAs and therefore nothing to be measured on. Do both.

Check your work at **Teams**, **Org chart**. **Edit reporting lines** there lets you fix every line in one place.

---

## Step 8: tag the cross-cutting groups (optional, but do it before step 9)

**Where:** create the tags in Workspace settings, **Task system**, **Tags**, then attach them on a person's record, in the **Tags** row.

Structure covers "which department" and "which job title". Tags cover everything that cuts across both: a cohort ("Q3 joiners"), a programme ("Onboarding"), a location shift, a task force.

**What this unlocks:** a tag can be the audience of a goal, a pulse survey or an announcement, and it filters the Directory. Membership is worked out live from the tag assignments, so someone tagged next month lands inside those audiences automatically, and someone untagged drops out.

---

## Step 9: set goals

**Where:** **Work**, then **Company goals**, **Team goals** or **My goals**, and **New goal**.

For each goal:

1. **Name** it.
2. **Owner**: the single accountable person. Only managers and above can assign a goal to someone else; everyone else's goals are owned by themselves.
3. **Contributors**: add people, whole departments, whole job titles, or a tag. Department, job title and tag contributors are worked out from their current members every time, so you do not have to maintain the list as the team changes.
4. **Level**: Company, Department or Individual.
5. **Quarter and dates.**
6. **Check-in cadence**: weekly, biweekly, monthly, or none. None silences that goal's check-in reminders.

Then open the goal and add **Targets** on the goal page: title, start value, target value, and an optional unit. Progress runs from the start value toward the target and rolls up from the targets automatically, and a goal with nothing measurable shows a dash rather than a fake 0%.

**Honest note:** linking a target to a KPI, so its number comes from the gauge instead of from hand check-ins, has no control in the interface. The goal page labels linked targets as measured by the KPI, but you cannot create the link from a screen today.

## Step 10: start the work structure

Alignment is now standing up. Now build where the work goes.

1. **Create a Space** per team, department, client or product line, from **New Space** in the Work sidebar. Leave **Restrict this Space** off and the Space is open to its members; turn it on and only the people you share it with can open it. To open it to the whole company, or to add people, use the Space's **Manage access**.
2. **Add Folders** inside the Space if you need a middle grouping. They nest up to 6 levels. Folders are optional; a List can hang straight off a Space.
3. **Create Lists** inside the Space or Folder. This is where tasks live.
4. **Set up each List**: its statuses, its fields, and its views. Board is the default view; add others from **+ View** and pin one as the default if you prefer it.
5. **Tag work to alignment** as you create tasks. The create-task dialog and the task drawer both carry a **KRA / KPI** field, and the task's Related section links the SOP that says how the work is done.

See [Spaces and tasks](/using/spaces-and-tasks).

## Step 11: turn on the measurement rhythm

Once people are placed and gauges exist, the recurring loop is:

- **Each person records their own numbers.** **Teams**, **My profile**, the **KRAs & KPIs** tab, **Record my numbers**. Available to anyone with at least one KRA.
- **Managers review them.** **Teams**, **KPI reviews** lists your reports with their readings, and approves or sends them back.
- **Goal owners check in** on their targets at the goal's cadence. A goal that goes past its cadence without a check-in nudges the owner in the bell and by email on weekday mornings.

---

## Setup checklist

Copy this into a task list and work down it.

- [ ] Departments created
- [ ] A job title exists for every distinct job, each with its description, department and seniority filled in
- [ ] Every job title has its KRAs, with weights that total 100 (check the sum)
- [ ] Every KRA has at least one KPI, with a written definition, unit, healthy line, direction, and an honest Owned or Shared setting
- [ ] At least one SOP exists, published, and linked to a KRA
- [ ] Everyone invited, with department, role and manager set in the dialog where you knew them
- [ ] Everyone placed: department, job title and **Reports to** confirmed on each record
- [ ] Org chart looks right under Teams, Org chart
- [ ] Cross-cutting tags created and attached to the right people
- [ ] Goals created with a single owner and resolved contributors, with targets added
- [ ] Spaces, Folders and Lists created; statuses, fields and views configured
- [ ] First KPI recording period run end to end, recorded and reviewed

---

## Known gaps in this path

These are real limits in the current build, not things you are doing wrong:

- Departments cannot be merged, only deleted once empty.
- KRA weight totals are not enforced, for a job title or for a person. You get a warning, not a refusal, and the weight does not yet weigh any score.
- There is no control in the interface to link a goal target to a KPI.
