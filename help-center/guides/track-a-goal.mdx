title: Track a goal
description: Create a goal, understand owner versus contributors, add a target with a start value, check in, and read the progress number correctly.
---

A goal in WorkwrK is one record with one accountable owner, any number of contributors, and one progress number. There are no per-person copies, so there is never a question about whose number is the real one.

Goals live in the Work sidebar, under `My goals`, `Team goals` and `Company goals`.

## 1. Create the goal

Use the `New goal` button in a level card's header, which presets that level.

| Field | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `Goal name` | Required. |
| `Owner` | The one person accountable. Only managers and above see this field; everyone else's goals are owned by them. |
| `Level` | `Company`, `Department` or `Individual`. Non-managers can only create `Individual` goals. |
| `Contributors` | People, departments, job titles and tags who can see and push the goal. |
| `Start date`, `End date` | Optional. |
| `Quarter` | Free text, for example `Q3 2026`. |
| `Check-in cadence` | `Weekly`, `Biweekly`, `Monthly` or `None`. |
| `Description` | Why the goal is set, and how it should be achieved. |

## 2. Owner versus contributors

This is the distinction that trips people up most, so it is worth being blunt about it.

- The **owner** is one person, and they are accountable. They show on the goal's page and in the owner column of the list. The owner, a manager in their chain, and Admins can edit the goal or check in on its targets.
- **Contributors** are the audience: who can see the goal and push it forward. You add them as individual people, whole departments, job titles, or tags.

Departments, job titles and tags are stored as references, not as a frozen list of names, and are worked out from their current members every time the goal is read. Move somebody into a department and they inherit that department's goals without anyone editing the goal. Take them out and they drop off.

Both feed visibility: a goal is visible to its owner, to its contributors, to a manager looking down their chain, and to Directors and above, the People team and Admins. Company goals are visible to everyone.

<Callout kind="alert">
  Adding contributors does not split the goal. There is one scoreboard, and everyone assigned shares the same progress bar. If two teams genuinely need separate numbers, create two goals.
</Callout>

## 3. Add a target, and mind the start value

A goal with no targets has nothing to measure, so this step is what turns an intention into something trackable. On the goal page, use `+ Add` on the `Targets` card, or `Create a Target` on the empty state.

| Field | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| `Target name` | What measurable result defines success. |
| `Start` | Where you are today. Defaults to `0`. |
| `Target` | Where you need to get to. Defaults to `100`. |
| `Unit` | Optional, for example `%`, `$` or `users`. |

The **start value is not cosmetic**. Progress is measured from start to target, not from zero. A target that runs from 40 to 60 is at 0% when the current value is 40 and at 50% when it is 50. If you leave `Start` at 0 when you are really beginning at 40, the target will look two-thirds finished on the day it is created.

The direction is inferred from the two numbers. If `Target` is lower than `Start`, WorkwrK understands the target as one you are driving down, and a falling number counts as progress.

## 4. Check in

Click a target row, or choose `Check in` from its `…` menu. The check-in dialog shows the target's current percentage, its `Start`, `Current` and `Target` values, then:

1. Choose `Increase` or `Decrease`.
2. Type the amount of the change, not the new total. The dialog previews the result underneath, as "current becomes new".
3. Optionally add a `Note`, up to 2000 characters, to record why the number moved.
4. Press `Save update`.

Each target row shows when it was last checked in, or "no check-ins yet".

<Callout kind="info">
  If a target is measured by a KPI, the dialog says so and does not accept a manual check-in. Record the KPI reading instead and the number arrives on the goal automatically. This keeps one number in one place rather than two that can disagree.
</Callout>

## 5. How progress is calculated

Three simple rules, applied in order.

1. **A target's progress** is how far the current value has travelled from `Start` towards `Target`, as a percentage, clamped between 0 and 100.
2. **A goal's progress** is the average of its targets. Sub-goals roll up too: a parent goal averages its own targets together with its measured children. Children that have nothing to measure contribute nothing, so three empty sub-goals cannot drag a measured sibling's 60% down.
3. **Status follows progress**: 100% or more is `Completed`, 70% or more is `On track`, 40% or more is `At risk`, and anything below that is `Behind`.

A goal with no targets and no measured children has no computed progress. It shows whatever progress it was given, and the list flags it as unmeasured rather than pretending it is at 0%.

## Everyday use

- `My goals` narrows the list to goals you carry, as owner or as a contributor. That narrowing happens on the server, so it is a genuine filter.
- The Goals page groups goals into `Company Goals`, `Department Goals` and `Individual Goals`, with a stats strip for average progress, on track, needs attention and completed.
- `Check-in cadence` drives check-in reminders. Setting it to `None` silences reminders for that goal.
- Deleting a target deletes its check-in history with it, and the goal's progress is recalculated from what remains.
