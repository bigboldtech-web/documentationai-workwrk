A goal in WorkwrK is one record with **one accountable owner**, **many contributors**, and **one progress number**. There are no per-person copies of a shared goal, so there is never a question about whose number is the real one.

Goals live in the **Work** sidebar, under **My goals**, **Team goals** and **Company goals**.

---

## The Goals page

The page opens with a stats strip (average progress, on track, needs attention, completed), then one card per level:

- **Company Goals**: what the whole company is pushing toward
- **Department Goals**: how each department supports the company goals
- **Individual Goals**: what each person commits to this cycle

Each goal is a row linking to its detail page: name, level chip, quarter or due date, the contributor avatar stack, the owner's avatar, and a thin progress bar with a percentage.

**My goals** narrows the list to goals you carry, either as owner or as a resolved contributor. That filtering happens on the server, so it is a real narrowing, not a client-side hide.

Hover a row for its `…` menu, or right-click it. The menu offers **Open**, **Edit goal**, **Assign owner**, **Copy link** and **Delete goal**. Edit, Assign owner and Delete only appear when you are actually allowed to do them, matching what the API will accept.

## Creating a goal

Use the **New goal** button in a level card's header, which presets that level.

The modal asks for:

| Field | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| **Goal name** | Required. |
| **Owner** | "Who is responsible for this Goal?" Only managers and above see this field; everyone else's goals are owned by them. |
| **Level** | Company, Department or Individual. There is no Team level. Non-managers can only create Individual goals. |
| **Contributors** | People, departments, roles and tags, in one picker with a section per kind. Departments, roles and tags resolve to their current members. |
| **Start date** and **End date** | Optional. |
| **Quarter** | Free text, for example `Q3 2026`. |
| **Check-in cadence** | Weekly, Biweekly, Monthly, or None. Drives the overdue-check-in warning and the reminders below. **None** is the per-goal off switch: it silences that goal's check-in reminders entirely, and the modal says so when you pick it. |
| **Description** | Why the goal is set and how it should be achieved. |

The same modal is used for editing. Open it from the `…` menu on a row or from the goal detail header.

### Owner versus contributors

This distinction is the point of the model, so it is worth stating plainly:

- The **owner** is a single person. They are accountable. They appear in the hero of the goal page and in the owner column on the list.
- **Contributors** are the audience: who can see the goal and push it. You add them as individual people, as whole departments, as roles, or as **tags**. Departments, roles and tags are stored as references and resolved to their **current** active members whenever the goal is read. Move someone into a department and they inherit that department's goals without anyone editing the goal. Stick a tag on someone and the same thing happens.

The tag option is the one to reach for when the group is not a department and not a job title: a cohort, a task force, a programme, a shift. Create the tag in Workspace settings, **Task system**, **Tags**, attach it to people on their record, then name the tag as a contributor here. Archived tags stop resolving.

Both feed the same visibility rules: a goal is visible to its owner, to its resolved contributors, to a manager looking down their chain, and to Directors and above, the People team and Admins. Company goals are visible to everyone. Guessing the address of a goal you are not entitled to see shows a not found page, not a redirect.

## The goal detail page

The hero band carries the large progress ring, the title with its `…` menu (right-click anywhere in the hero opens the same menu), the description, and meta chips: level, status, quarter, date range, and cadence. On the right: due date, owner, and the resolved contributor stack, which is also the editor for contributors if you have edit rights.

Below the hero:

- **Targets**: the key results (see next section)
- **Timeline**: the check-in history, newest first, with the target name, the new value, any note, and who logged it
- **Cascades from**: the parent goal, shown only if you are allowed to see it
- **Cascades to**: child goals, each showing its own rolled-up number
- **On track?**: an automated assessment (see below)
- **Effort**: the real work moving the goal (see below)
- **Linked work**: Spaces, Boards and KRAs attached to this goal
- **Custom fields**

If the goal has not been checked in within its cadence, an amber banner appears at the top of the cards saying how long it has been.

The same staleness also drives reminders, unless the cadence is None. On weekday mornings, every goal that has an owner and is not complete is checked: when a goal has gone past its cadence window without a check-in, a notification lands in the owner's bell and an email follows. The email honours the owner's notification settings, so someone who has turned off reminder emails is nudged in the bell only. A goal set to the None cadence is skipped entirely, no bell and no email.

### Linked work drives effort, not the progress number

Attaching a KRA, a Board or a Space to a goal records the connection so you can jump from the objective to the projects moving it. It does **not** feed the progress number: only targets do. It does, however, feed the **Effort** and **On track?** signals below, which read the real work under those links.

## Targets (key results)

A target is a measurable result: a name, a **Start** value, a **Target** value, and an optional **unit** such as `%`, `$` or `users`. Add one from **+ Add** on the Targets card, or **Create a Target** from the empty state.

Start is not decoration. Progress is measured from the start value toward the target value, not from zero, so a target that runs 40 to 60 reads 50% when it hits 50. It is also what makes a "reduce it" target work: when the target sits below the start, progress rises as the number falls.

Each target row shows the owner's avatar, the title, a progress bar with the current-over-target fraction, and a `…` menu with **Check in** and **Delete target**.

### Checking in

Click a row, or **Check in** from its menu, to open the check-in dialog. It shows the target's progress bar and its Start / Current / Target values, then asks for a change:

1. Choose **Increase** or **Decrease**.
2. Type the amount. This is a delta against the current value, not the new total; the dialog does the arithmetic and previews the resulting value before you save.
3. Add an optional note.
4. **Save update**.

The check-in lands in the goal's Timeline and the ring repaints.

### Targets measured by a KPI

A target can be linked to a role KPI. When it is, **the gauge is the measurement**: the target reads the KPI's latest recorded value at the moment you load the page, and hand check-ins are refused by the server.

Those targets are labelled as derived and the check-in dialog shows an amber notice up front telling you to record the KPI reading instead. If you try anyway, the same notice repeats the server's answer.

Nothing you typed is destroyed by this. A hand-entered value stays stored on the target; it simply is not what the UI reads while the KPI link exists.

**Honest note:** there is no control anywhere in the interface that creates this link. Everything downstream of the link works correctly, but today the link can only be made through the API.

## Where the one number comes from

Progress is derived when the page is read, not stored and re-shown. The rule:

1. If the goal has targets, the number rolls up from them. Each target's own progress runs from its start value toward its target value, honouring direction, so a target that improves by going **down** rises as the value falls.
2. If the goal has no targets but has child goals that are measured, it rolls up from those children. The rollup uses org-wide context, so a parent is still correct even when you cannot see every child.
3. If a number was set by hand and nothing else measures the goal, that hand-set number is shown.
4. If nothing measures the goal at all, it shows a dash, not `0%`.

That last rule is deliberate. A brand-new goal with no targets is not "0% done and behind", it is "not measured yet", and the product says so.

Status follows the same source. While a goal is measured, On track / At risk / Behind / Completed is derived from the rolled-up progress rather than read from a stale column.

Because it is derived in one place, the same number appears on the Goals list, on the goal detail ring, in the "Cascades to" rows on the parent, and on the Goals tab of your record. If they ever disagreed, one of them would be lying, so they do not disagree.

## Effort behind the goal

The **Effort** card answers a different question from progress: how much real work is actually moving this goal, and who is doing it. It is never self-reported. Every number is derived from the work linked to the goal:

- Tasks under the goal's linked **KRAs** (their logged hours and done-versus-open counts).
- Items in the goal's linked **Boards** and **Spaces**, with time read from the unified time-tracking entries on those cards.

The card shows hours logged, tasks done, tasks in progress, when it last moved, and a short list of who is contributing. If nothing is linked yet, it says so and nudges you to link a KRA, Board or Space. Link a busy board to a goal and its hours, tasks and contributors flow in automatically.

---

## On track? (automated assessment)

The **On track?** card gives a plain verdict, **On track**, **At risk** or **Off track**, with a one line reason and one recommended next step. The verdict is computed from the goal's real signals, not guessed:

- The rolled-up progress and status.
- **Pace**: how far along the goal is versus how much of its time window has elapsed, and how many days are left.
- Whether check-ins have gone stale against the cadence.
- The effort above (hours and tasks from linked work).

The verdict itself is deterministic and consistent with the same thresholds used everywhere else, so it never contradicts the number on the ring. When AI is available it writes the human explanation on top of that verdict; when it is not, a plain auto-generated summary stands in. Either way the card degrades gracefully and never blocks the page.
