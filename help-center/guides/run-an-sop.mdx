---
title: Write and run an SOP
description: Choose the right SOP kind, write it, publish it, assign it, and follow the acknowledgements through to Compliance.
---

An SOP in WorkwrK goes through four states that matter to people: it is written, published, assigned, and acknowledged. Compliance is simply the arithmetic of that last step.

## 1. Pick the right kind

Go to `SOPs` in the Docs hub and press `New SOP`. Four kinds are offered, and the choice decides which editor you get.

| Kind | Use it when | What you get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Written SOP` | A policy, runbook or process that reads as prose | A notes-style block editor, versioned on every save |
| `Step-by-step SOP` | A recipe someone reads top to bottom | An ordered step list with per-step notes and screenshots |
| `Checklist SOP` | Discrete steps someone ticks off as they go | A step list that can be run and tracked, with a share link |
| `Click-capture SOP` | A click path through a piece of software | A screenshot and a written step per click, captured by the recorder extension |

The sidebar `+` on the SOPs app offers the same four directly: `New written SOP`, `New step-by-step SOP`, `New checklist SOP` and `New click-capture SOP`.

Click-capture works differently from the other three: it is recorded by a browser extension rather than typed. See [The SOP recorder isn't capturing](/help-center/troubleshooting/sop-extension-capture).

## 2. Write it

Written and Checklist SOPs open their own editor and auto-save as you work; the header shows `Saving…` and then the time of the last save.

- A **Written SOP** has a title, a description under it ("what is this SOP for, and when should someone reach for it?"), and the block editor for the body.
- A **Checklist SOP** has a title and a builder of sections and steps. The header counts the steps for you.
- A **Step-by-step SOP** is created and opens directly in inline editing on the SOP page.

## 3. Publish it

Press `Publish`. On the SOP page it also sits under the `…` menu. Publishing increments the version and makes the SOP available to the team.

There is a light approval path if you want one, in the `…` menu on the SOP page. A `Draft` can be moved to `Submit for review`; something in review can be `Approve` or `Request changes`, which sends it back to draft.

<Callout kind="alert">
  Publishing is a separate permission from creating. If you cannot publish, your SOP is still saved as a `Draft` for someone who can. Publishing is also validated: a blank title or empty content is refused, and you cannot blank the title or body of an already published SOP. Archive it instead.
</Callout>

## 4. Assign it

Assignment is only offered once the SOP is published. From the SOP page, open the `…` menu and choose `Assign`.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose who" icon="users">
    `Assign to Department` assigns everyone in a department at once. `Or Select Individuals` lets you tick people one by one; anyone already assigned is shown greyed out as `Already assigned`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set a due date" icon="calendar">
    `Due Date` is optional. It is what makes an assignment count as overdue later.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Decide if it is mandatory" icon="badge-alert">
    `Mandatory` defaults to `Yes`. Mandatory overdue SOPs are called out separately on each person's `My SOPs` page.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Assign" icon="check">
    Press `Assign SOP`. Everyone assigned gets a notification linking to `My SOPs`, and an email if their SOP email preference is on.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Removing an assignment later keeps that person's compliance history; it only takes the SOP off their list.

New joiners are a special case: when their invitation carries a job title, the published SOPs under that title's KRAs are assigned the moment they join, so they arrive with reading already waiting. See [How do I invite my team?](/help-center/faq/how-to-invite-team).

## 5. How a person acknowledges it

Assignees have two routes, and both do the same thing.

- On the SOP page, an assignee sees a `Mark as read` button in the header. Once used, it becomes `Acknowledged` with the date.
- On `My SOPs`, in the Docs hub, every outstanding row carries an `Acknowledge` button.

`My SOPs` groups assignments into `Overdue`, `Active` and `Completed`, with tiles counting overdue, active, completed and the percentage of steps done. Overdue mandatory items get a banner at the top.

Acknowledging is idempotent. Pressing it twice does not double-count.

## 6. Watch Compliance

Open `SOP compliance` in the Docs hub. The dashboard reads acknowledgements, so it only becomes meaningful once people start marking things as read. It shows:

- **Org rate**, the share of all assignments completed, plus counts of assignments, in-progress and overdue
- **By department**, a completion bar per department
- **Lowest-completion SOPs**, so you can see which procedure is being ignored
- **Top performers**, by personal completion rate
- **Overdue**, each late assignment with the person and how many days late

Compliance requires manager access. Below that tier it reports that manager access is required rather than showing an empty dashboard.

<Callout kind="tip">
  A Compliance page stuck at 0% usually means the SOPs were assigned but nobody has acknowledged them yet. Point people at `My SOPs`; the button is right on the row.
</Callout>

## Extras worth knowing

- **Checklist runs.** A published Checklist SOP gets a `Start run` button. A run has its own title, an optional assignee and due date, and produces a share link so someone outside the workspace can complete it. Runs are listed under `Run history` in the Docs hub.
- **Walk through.** Any SOP with steps can be played one step at a time from the `…` menu. Prose SOPs do not offer it, because there would be no steps to play.
- **Public link.** A published SOP can expose a `Copy public link`, while your workspace allows public links in Workspace settings, `Access`. The same menu can turn that link off again.
- **Archive.** Archiving hides an SOP from active lists and is reversible.
