**My profile** is the first row in the Teams sidebar, and **My profile** in your avatar menu goes to the same place. It is your own record: everyone in the company has one of the same shape.

It answers three questions in one place: what am I accountable for, how is it reading, and what is the record. Your name, photo and contact details, your data download and account deletion live in **My settings**, **Profile** instead. See [Settings](/admin/settings).

---

## One record, shown by who is looking

The same record opens for everyone, with only the tabs the viewer is allowed to see:

- **Everyone** at the company sees the card at the top (name, job title, department, who you report to) and the **Overview** and **Skills** tabs.
- **You**, your reporting chain, the People team, Directors and above, and Owners and Admins also see **KRAs & KPIs**, **Goals**, **Reviews**, **Assets**, and your score.
- **Kudos** shows to everyone but Guests, and **Reports** shows when you have direct reports.

The same rule is checked again when the data is read, so a colleague cannot pull your numbers by other means.

---

## The card and your tags

The card carries your photo, name, job title, department, office and who you report to. **Edit profile** on your own record changes your personal fields. Your job title, department, office and manager are a management decision: the record says **Ask your manager or the People team to change your job title, department, office or manager.**

Under the card, **Overview** shows what your job title is for and, for the people who may see it, your score. Your **Tags** are there too. A tag is a label from the workspace list, used for the cuts the org chart does not make: a launch team, a shift, a cohort. Tags matter because a goal can list a tag as a contributor, and a pulse survey or an announcement can be aimed at one. The people who manage your record set them; you can read yours.

## KRAs & KPIs

The **KRAs & KPIs** tab is the core of the record.

KRAs come **with your job title**. You do not create them here. If yours is empty, the tab says **Ask your manager to assign KRAs.**

Each KRA shows its name, its weight, its description, and then one row per KPI underneath. Each KPI row shows:

| Element | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Star | This is the north-star gauge. |
| Direction arrow | Higher is better, lower is better, or hold the line. |
| **Shared** chip | A shared gauge: you influence it and it is reviewed, but you are not graded on it. No chip means you own it. |
| Coloured dot | Health against the healthy line. |
| Value | Your latest reading and its period, or **No reading**. |
| Target | The healthy line, or "no baseline yet" when the KPI has none. |
| Status chip | Where this period's reading stands: Not recorded, Submitted, Approved or Changes requested. |

Two honesty rules are built into this display. A KPI with no healthy line reads **no baseline yet** rather than inventing a line, and a KPI with no reading reads **No reading** rather than showing a zero.

### Recording your numbers

**Record my numbers** opens the recorder on the tab. Pick a period, expand a KRA, and type the actual value for each KPI, with an optional note. A qualitative KPI takes a 1 to 5 rating instead.

Saving a self-report sends it to your manager as **Submitted**. It is not final until your manager signs off, which is why the status chip moves to Approved or Changes requested. Your note travels with it.

A manager recording numbers on a report's behalf uses **Record numbers** on that person's record, and can open **Manage alignment** there to adjust KRAs, weights and SOPs.

**History**, below the KRAs, lists past readings: the KPI, its period, the score, and actual over target. A reading awaiting sign-off shows Pending instead of a score.

## Goals

The **Goals** tab lists the open goals you carry, each with the same On track verdict the Goals pages show. You carry a goal either because you own it or because you are a contributor: as yourself, through your department, your job title, or one of your tags.

The number here is the same derived rollup the Goals pages show: it reads as a dash when nothing about the goal is measured yet, not 0%.

---

## Reviews

The **Reviews** tab links to **My weekly review** and lists your review-cycle results, with the cycle name and who reviewed you. A completed review offers its appraisal letter.

### My weekly review

**My weekly review** is the heartbeat. It opens the current week's review, creating a draft if one does not exist, and gives you:

- a progress slider for each of your active KRAs
- a snapshot for each KPI under those KRAs
- **Highlights** and **Blockers**

You can **Save draft** or **Submit**. The page shows where the week stands and your manager's state: pending, approved or changes requested.

## Skills, kudos, assets and reports

| Tab | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| **Skills** | Your skills, with your own rating and your manager's when one exists. **Add skill** and **Edit rating** on your own record; your manager can **Rate** and **Remove**. |
| **Kudos** | Kudos you have received, with reactions. On a colleague's record, **Give kudos**. |
| **Assets** | Equipment assigned to you. |
| **Reports** | Only when you have direct reports: each one as a link, and **Open in Org chart**. |

## Your assigned SOPs

Procedures assigned to you live in the **Docs** hub under **My SOPs**, grouped into overdue, active and done.

Closing one out is yours to do, not your manager's: **Acknowledge** on the row, or **Mark as read** in the header of the SOP itself, completes the assignment and stamps the date. The SOP header then reads **Acknowledged** with that date. Neither control needs any management permission, so an ordinary Member always has a way to finish what they were assigned.
